# 7. EQ

EQ is the fourth module in the default chain. It has four bands: LOW, MID, TONE and HIGH. Each
band can be a shelf or a bell. The EQ adds no latency.

![EQ card on the chain strip](images/main-editor.png)

## Controls

Values are the defaults of a new instance.

| Control | Range and units | Default | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| **EQ** chip | on or off | on | Enables the module. |
| Mode | ST, MS | ST | Stereo mode. In ST the bands act on left and right. In MS they act on mid and side. |
| Curve | Clean, Proportional | Clean | Band Q behaviour. See below. |
| Per band: **on** | on or off | on (all four bands) | Enables the band. |
| Per band: Type | see the table below | LOW and HIGH: Shelf. MID and TONE: Wide | Filter shape. |
| Per band: Freq | 20 Hz to 20 kHz | LOW 100 Hz, MID 500 Hz, TONE 2.5 kHz, HIGH 10 kHz | Centre frequency, or corner frequency for a shelf. |
| Per band: Gain | −18 to +18 dB | 0 dB | Boost or cut. |
| Per band: Q | 0.2 to 10 | LOW 0.7, MID 1.0, TONE 1.0, HIGH 0.7 | Bandwidth. **Used only by Bell and Param types.** See the note below. |
| Per band: Target | Both, Mid / L, Side / R | Both | Channel the band acts on. In MS mode these are Mid and Side. In ST mode they are L and R. |

### Band types

| Band | Type | Shape | Q |
|---|---|---|---|
| LOW and HIGH | Shelf | Shelf | Fixed at 0.71 |
| LOW and HIGH | Res Shelf | Shelf with resonance | Fixed at 1.5 |
| LOW and HIGH | Bell | Bell | The Q knob |
| MID and TONE | Wide | Bell | Fixed at 0.55 (MID) and 0.8 (TONE) |
| MID and TONE | Narrow | Bell | Fixed at 2.5 |
| MID and TONE | Param | Bell | The Q knob |

**Note on Q.** The Q knob is live on every band in the editor, but it only changes the sound for
Bell and Param. A shelf, a Wide band and a Narrow band ignore it. Change the type to Param or Bell
before you set Q.

### Curve: Clean and Proportional

- **Clean.** Q is the value you set, for every gain.
- **Proportional.** The effective Q rises with the gain of the band, as on a console. A bell at ±18 dB
  has 2.5 times the Q you set. A shelf at ±18 dB has 1.25 times.

## Display

The large curve is the static response of the four bands, plus the analyser:

- **Handles.** Each band has a handle at its frequency and gain. Drag a handle to change the
  frequency and gain. Hold Shift for a 10 % move.
- **Q.** The mouse wheel over a handle changes the Q. Shift gives a fine step. The rings around a
  handle show the bandwidth.
- **Reset gain.** Double-click a handle to set that band's gain back to 0 dB.
- **Mid and Side.** In MS mode the display shows two curves, one for mid and one for side.
- **Analyser.** The spectrum before and after the EQ, with a 4096-point FFT and 75 % overlap. The
  display tilts the spectrum by 4.5 dB per octave, around 1 kHz. Use the freeze button to hold the
  display.

The display matches the processing to within 0.001 dB with dynamic gain applied (`eq_displayMatchesMeasuredResponse`).

## Dynamic bands (host only)

Each band can be dynamic. A dynamic band adds its own gain change on top of its static gain, driven
by the level in that band. Its controls are:

| Parameter | Range | Default |
|---|---|---|
| Dynamic on | on or off | off |
| Dyn Threshold | −60 to 0 dB | −30 dB |
| Dyn Range | −18 to +18 dB | −6 dB |
| Dyn Speed | Fast, Medium, Slow | Medium |
| Dyn External SC | on or off | off |

Fast is 2 ms attack and 50 ms release. Medium is 8 ms and 150 ms. Slow is 30 ms and 500 ms.

**The editor has no control for these.** They are visible in the display, and a host can set them
through its parameter list or automation. The EQ display draws the dynamic gain when it is set.

## Measured behaviour

From `docs/dsp/eq.md`. The numbers come from `docs/measurements/eq.md`.

- **Static accuracy.** Shelves are within 0.31 dB of the analog prototype at 48 kHz, and bells within
  0.41 dB at 48 kHz for centre frequencies up to 0.4 × the sample rate. The worst case in the full grid
  is 0.50 dB, for a Proportional bell at 44.1 kHz.
- **Switching.** A change of band type or target crossfades over 15 ms. Splatter for that change is
  −76 to −133 dB against a 100 Hz or 1 kHz tone.
- **Determinism.** The output is identical at every block size from 1 to 2048, with automation.

## Latency and CPU

- Latency is 0 samples, and it does not change with any setting.
- At 48 kHz, four static bands cost 0.06 % of a core. Four dynamic bands cost 0.14 % with steady
  signals, against a budget of 0.15 %. With gains gliding, four dynamic bands cost 0.80 % at 48 kHz,
  against a target of 1 %.

## Limits

- The Q knob is live for Shelf, Res Shelf, Wide and Narrow, and it has no effect there. See above.
- The dynamic bands and band solo are host only. Band solo is the `eq_solo` parameter. It is
  transient, so it is not saved with a preset.
- A dynamic band below its threshold, with a static gain of 0 dB, does no processing. It nulls
  exactly. Below the threshold with a static gain, it is bit-identical to the same band without
  dynamics.
- A detector bias: on a steady pure tone at the band frequency, a dynamic band reads about 0.12 dB more
  reduction than the law gives. Program material does not show it as a steady offset.
- At 96 kHz, a dynamic band that is gliding costs 1.60 % of a core. This is above the 1 % target.
